❓ Halal Product Scanner — Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Everything you need to know about our food screening process, AI optical reading, and Halal classification standards.

📌

1. General & Purpose

What is the ASM Halal Product Scanner?
The ASM Halal Product Scanner is an automated screening tool developed for students and the community in Italy to quickly inspect food ingredients, detect prohibited (Haram) or doubtful (Mashbuh) additives, and verify Halal compliance against Italian and EU food labeling standards.
Is this scanner an official religious ruling (Fatwa)?
No. The scanner is an automated decision-support tool. It analyzes ingredient texts and known additives against established Islamic dietary guidelines. Manufacturers frequently change ingredient formulations, sourcing, and production lines. When in doubt, consult product packaging directly or ask a qualified scholar.
🔍

2. How to Use the Scanner

What are the two main ways to check a product?

The scanner offers two primary screening modes:

  • Barcode Mode:
    • Camera: Uses your device camera to scan EAN-13 / UPC barcodes in real-time.
    • Manual: Lets you type in the barcode numbers directly.
  • Ingredients Mode:
    • Photo (AI Vision): Take a picture or upload a packaging photo of the ingredients list; AI is used only to extract and read the ingredients, and then our system classifies it.
    • Paste Text: Paste or type the ingredients list manually in Italian, English, or French for instant cross-examination.
Does AI decide whether a product is Halal or Haram?
No, AI does NOT decide the Halal/Haram status. Artificial intelligence is used only to extract and read the raw ingredients and E-numbers from your packaging photo (Optical Character Recognition / OCR). Once the text is extracted, our own verified rule-based system inspects and classifies each ingredient against scholarly Islamic dietary guidelines. This ensures deterministic, verified classification rather than AI hallucinations or guesses.
Does the scanner store my camera feed or personal data?
No. Camera access operates locally in your web browser via secure WebRTC/getUserMedia protocols to detect barcodes or capture single ingredient photos. No live video stream is transmitted or saved.
🏷️

3. Classification & Results

What do the different status badges mean?
  • 🟢 Halal (Permissible / Certificato Halal): No prohibited or questionable ingredients were detected. If the product holds recognized Halal certification, this will also be highlighted.
  • 🔴 Haram (Prohibited): Contains ingredients or derivatives strictly prohibited in Islam (e.g., pork derivatives, animal gelatin of non-Halal origin, carmine/cochineal dye, added alcohol).
  • 🟡 Mashbuh (Doubtful / Needs Verification): Contains substances that could be of either animal or plant/synthetic origin (e.g., mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids E471, pepsin, enzymes, tallow derivatives) without specifying vegetable origin.
  • ⚪ Not Listed / Needs Review (Non in Lista): The barcode is not currently in Open Food Facts or our database, or no ingredients list is available to analyze.
🧪

4. Ingredients & Additives Explained

Why was my product flagged as Haram for “Gelatin” or “E441”?
Gelatin (gelatina, gélatine) in European products is predominantly derived from pork skin/bones unless explicitly certified as Halal beef gelatin (gelatina bovina halal) or fish gelatin (gelatina di pesce). Any general or porcine gelatin is flagged as Haram.
Why is E120 (Carmine / Cochineal) flagged as Haram?
E120 (Carminio, Cochineal, Acid Carminic) is a red food dye extracted from crushed insects (Dactylopius coccus). Under the majority scholarly consensus, insect-derived coloring is not permissible for consumption.
How does the scanner handle alcohol and vinegar?
  • Flagged (Haram): Added drinking alcohols, ethyl alcohol (alcol etilico), wine, rum, liqueurs, brandy, and ethanol used as flavor carriers or preservatives in baked goods (e.g., tramezzini, bread, cakes).
  • Allowed (Halal): Naturally fermented vinegars (aceto di vino, aceto balsamico, cider vinegar), sugar alcohols/polyols (such as sorbitol, maltitol, xylitol, erythritol), and non-food cetyl/stearyl alcohols.
What about E471 (Mono- and Diglycerides of Fatty Acids)?
E471 and related emulsifiers (E472a–f) can be synthesized from plant oils or animal fats. Unless the label specifies “100% vegetale” (vegetable origin), it will be marked as Mashbuh (doubtful).
⚠️

5. Troubleshooting & Limitations

Why does the scanner say “Product Not Listed / Da Verificare”?
The database relies on Open Food Facts and ASM's verified database. If a brand or generic store product has not been registered yet, switch to the Ingredients tab, snap a photo of the ingredients list, or paste the text directly.
What should I do if the AI misread an ingredient photo?
  • Ensure the packaging text is well-lit, flat, and in sharp focus without glare.
  • If text was missed, tap “Paste text” and copy the ingredients directly.
  • Use the 🚩 “Think result is incorrect? Flag for review” button on the result card to submit a quick note to the ASM administration team.
Can I suggest new products or get help with complex ingredients?
Yes! You can:
  • Flag any product directly within the scanner results for review.
  • Join the Halal Scanner WhatsApp Group via the button below the scanner.
  • Use the “Ask an Imam” link on the page to submit complex dietary inquiries to scholars.
